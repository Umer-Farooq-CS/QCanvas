CORE THESIS (carry this through every section)
═══════════════════════════════════════════════

Prior cross-framework comparisons are methodologically flawed: each
framework runs on its own native simulator, so observed differences
conflate transpiler quality with simulator quality. This paper resolves
that confound by introducing a unified pipeline — QCanvas compiles every
circuit from its native framework to OpenQASM 3.0, and all circuits
execute on the same QSim backend. Any measured difference is therefore
attributable solely to the framework's transpilation strategy and gate
vocabulary. Using this pipeline across 12 algorithms, 3 frameworks
(Qiskit, Cirq, PennyLane), and up to 8 qubits, we find that:
  (a) Qiskit and Cirq are virtually equivalent in circuit quality
      (S_overall 58.80 vs 58.79);
  (b) PennyLane diverges significantly, especially for oracle/search
      algorithms where it exhibits super-linear gate scaling (a=2.21 for
      Grover's, producing 216 gates at 6 qubits vs 18 for Qiskit/Cirq);
  (c) statistical equivalence breaks down systematically — not randomly —
      whenever PennyLane uses a different gate basis, revealing a
      decomposition incompatibility rather than noise.

    - Quantum Teleportation: Cirq 12 gates, Qiskit 8, PennyLane 6.
    - Bit-Flip Code: Cirq 9 vs Qiskit/PennyLane 10.
    - Phase-Flip Code: Cirq 15 vs Qiskit/PennyLane 16.

  Circuit depth headline:
    Qiskit produces the shallowest or equal-depth circuits for all 12
    algorithms. Notable findings:
    - QAOA (2q): PennyLane depth 3 vs Qiskit 8 vs Cirq 9 (PennyLane's
      shallower circuit is a consequence of fewer gate types, not

    measurement basis. PennyLane's decomposition for multi-qubit

    Bit-Flip Code 3q: Q↔C JSD=0.707 (✗), Q↔PL JSD=0.000 (✓)

SECTION 6 — DISCUSSION  (~1.5 pages)
═══════════════════════════════════════════════

6.1 Framework Selection Guidance
  For NISQ practitioners: Qiskit and Cirq are functionally
  interchangeable in circuit quality terms (0.01 score gap). Selection
  between them should be based on ecosystem fit (IBM hardware → Qiskit;
  Google hardware → Cirq) or team preference, not performance. Both
  outperform PennyLane on hardware-efficiency metrics for oracle and
  search algorithms.

  PennyLane's advantage lies outside this study's scope: its
  automatic differentiation and device-agnostic design make it the
  preferred choice for variational algorithm research (VQE, QAOA,
  QML), even though its compiled circuits are larger.

6.2 The Decomposition Incompatibility Problem
  The JSD=1.000 failures are not compilation errors — PennyLane
  produces syntactically valid, executable circuits. The issue is
  semantic: PennyLane uses a different gate basis for multi-qubit
  oracle circuits at scale, which changes the computational basis in
  which results are measured. This is an underdocumented behaviour with
  serious implications: a practitioner who ports an algorithm from
  Qiskit to PennyLane at 5+ qubits and compares results will observe
  completely non-overlapping distributions and may incorrectly conclude
  their implementation is wrong.

6.3 The Unified Pipeline as a Methodological Contribution
  Emphasise that the JSD=1.000 findings are only discoverable because
  all circuits run on the same backend. Had we used PennyLane's native
  simulator alongside Qiskit's Aer, we could not distinguish framework-
  induced divergence from simulator-induced divergence.

6.4 Limitations
  - Classical simulation only (no physical hardware validation)
  - QSim backend uses statevector simulation; density matrix and
    stabilizer modes not evaluated
  - PennyLane version pinned to benchmark snapshot; decomposition
    strategy may change in future releases
  - Quantum Volume is structure-implied from a depolarizing model;
    does not reflect real hardware noise profiles (crosstalk, leakage)
  - 12-algorithm suite is representative but not exhaustive; more
    complex circuits (QAOA at deeper layers, fault-tolerant codes) are
    out of scope for this study

WRITING STYLE RULES (enforce throughout)
═══════════════════════════════════════════════

1. Every quantitative claim must include the number:
   BAD:  "PennyLane produces significantly more gates."
   GOOD: "PennyLane produces 216 gates at 6 qubits vs 18 for Qiskit
          and Cirq (a 12× overhead)."

2. Cite figures/tables immediately after the claim they support,
   not at the end of the paragraph.

3. LNCS uses numbered, non-bold section headings. Subsections use
   the format "4.1 Title" not "4.1. Title".

4. Avoid hedging unless genuinely uncertain:
   BAD:  "This may suggest that PennyLane could potentially..."
   GOOD: "PennyLane exhibits super-linear gate scaling (a=2.21)."

5. JSD=1.000 is your strongest finding — do not understate it. It
   means the distributions share zero support. Frame it precisely.

6. The QAOA PennyLane fidelity result (0.979 vs 0.919) MUST be
   explained in the correct direction: PennyLane's higher fidelity
   is because its circuit is shallower (fewer gates to accumulate
   error), not because PennyLane is more accurate. If not explained,
   a reader will draw the wrong conclusion.

7. Do not use "novel", "state-of-the-art", or "cutting-edge".
   Describe specifically what is new: "To our knowledge, this is
   the first study to route all circuits through a common OpenQASM
   3.0 intermediate representation before simulation..."

8. LNCS abstract has no section heading. Keywords appear immediately
   after, on a new line: "Keywords: keyword1 · keyword2 · keyword3"

9. Keep Related Work honest — acknowledge what others have done.
   Do not diminish prior work; show exactly where the gap is.

10. The paper's limitation section must include that these results
    are from classical simulation only. Do not claim hardware
    implications beyond what the depolarizing noise model supports.
